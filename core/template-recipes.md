# Template Recipes

These are original design recipes, not template files. They describe how to make a deck look and behave. They may be implemented in PPTX, HTML, or another slide tool.

When installed user collections exist or the user names a previously selected template, first use [the user-template set](../references/USER_TEMPLATE_SET.md). Its catalog and materialization helper provide actual selectable assets. The original recipes below remain useful when no selected asset fits the current content.

## 1. executive-consulting-dark

Best for:

- executive presentations;
- consulting-style recommendations;
- strategy reviews;
- transformation narratives.

Design rules:

- dark neutral background;
- restrained accent color;
- high-contrast conclusion titles;
- sparse text;
- structured charts, matrices, and roadmaps;
- no decorative gradients or stock-photo filler.

Use when the audience needs decisions, tradeoffs, and executive readability.

## 2. finance-control-room

Best for:

- finance business review;
- KPI review;
- budget and forecast discussion;
- operating dashboard narratives.

Design rules:

- dashboard-like grid;
- strong number hierarchy;
- compact tables only when needed;
- variance colors used sparingly;
- clear current-state, driver, risk, action sequence.

Use when the deck must connect data to management action.

## 3. academic-clean-lecture

Best for:

- university lectures;
- public courses;
- training sessions;
- concept explanation.

Design rules:

- bright neutral background;
- strong chapter structure;
- legible body text;
- diagrams and examples;
- room for speaker explanation;
- avoid over-designed business visuals.

Use when clarity and teaching rhythm matter more than executive compression.

## 4. product-demo-swiss

Best for:

- product launch;
- technical demo;
- software workflow explanation;
- architecture and system walkthrough.

Design rules:

- Swiss-style grid;
- one accent color;
- clean typography;
- screenshot-safe layouts;
- step-by-step demo pages;
- architecture diagrams with consistent labels.

Use when the deck must feel precise, modern, and product-led.

## 5. magazine-storytelling

Best for:

- opinion talks;
- industry observation;
- founder or expert speeches;
- narrative presentations.

Design rules:

- editorial rhythm;
- strong opening spread;
- image-led pages where rights are clear;
- pull quotes;
- chapter breaks;
- restrained motion for HTML decks.

Use when the goal is attention, memory, and narrative flow.

## 6. board-update-minimal

Best for:

- board updates;
- project steering committees;
- monthly or quarterly progress reviews.

Design rules:

- minimal visual system;
- status, decision, risk, and ask are explicit;
- no decorative pages;
- page titles state the conclusion;
- appendices carry detail.

Use when the audience needs fast orientation and clear asks.

## 7. blueprint-research

Best for:

- research-style presentations;
- NotebookLM-like visual benchmark requests;
- conceptual AI, finance, strategy, or technology topics;
- external talks where visual identity matters.

Design rules:

- blueprint, grid-paper, or technical-document visual world;
- structured diagrams instead of generic illustrations;
- muted blue-gray or off-white base;
- one warm or bright accent for key signals;
- strong page rhythm with concept maps, framework pages, system diagrams, and recap pages;
- avoid copying any generated or third-party deck images.

Use when the user wants a more distinctive, diagram-rich deck and accepts a hybrid route for the most visual pages.
